4. **Match the payment method.** Buyer and seller must be using the same rail for the trade to complete. If the ad says Mercado Pago and you send from a bank account, you've created a dispute before you've started.
5. **Pay the exact amount inside the window.** The advertiser sets a deadline between 15 and 90 minutes. Miss it and the order cancels automatically, your completion rate drops, and after three consecutive misses you lose the ability to cancel orders manually.
6. **Keep the transfer memo clean.** No BTC, no USDT, no "cripto" in the reference field. OKX explicitly warns that crypto wording in a bank memo can cause problems with the institution on the other end.
7. **Wait for release.** The seller releases from escrow after confirming receipt. If you're the seller, never release before the money is visible in your account, because traditional bank transfers are not instant.

## The September 2026 rules: what happens when someone asks for more money

This is the part most guides skip, and it's the part that decides whether a cheap-looking ad stays cheap. OKX now applies **zero tolerance to price negotiation**. Every trade must settle at the price and amount displayed on the order at the moment it was placed. The displayed price is fixed at order creation; the counterparty is the buyer if you're selling, or the seller if you're buying.

Four behaviours are prohibited outright:

- Asking the counterparty to negotiate or change the price after the order is placed
- Requesting extra payment or fees beyond the displayed order price
- Charging undisclosed transfer, conversion or other additional fees
- Intentionally paying less than the agreed order amount

**Underpayment.** An order for 1,000 USDT where the buyer sends the equivalent of 950 USDT and calls the difference a bank charge is treated as underpayment if the seller disputes it, whatever the buyer intended. If you spot a genuine discrepancy, message the seller through OKX chat first rather than sending a partial amount and explaining afterwards.

**Seller surcharges.** A seller who messages "I can only send it if you add 2% for the bank transfer fee" after the order is placed is in violation even though the listed price was never changed. If your costs move after you've posted an ad, OKX's guidance is to cancel the order and repost at the new price instead of asking the buyer to cover the gap mid-trade.

## Escrow, disputes and what OKX will actually do

The reason a P2P trade isn't a straight bet on a stranger is escrow. OKX holds the seller's crypto while the buyer pays off-platform, and releases it only when the seller confirms the fiat arrived. The counterparty risk sits on the payment rail, not on the exchange holding your USDT.

If something goes wrong, either side can open a dispute. OKX's customer support team mediates, reviews the material both parties submit plus anything else it considers necessary, and reserves the right to make the final call. Accounts engaged in harmful or fraudulent behaviour, including coordinated scams and malicious trading, can be frozen.

A few practical habits reduce your exposure. Check the merchant's completion rate before you trade. Prefer verified advertisers with the blue V when you're new, since OKX has already reviewed them. When selling, confirm the balance in your own account before releasing, particularly with bank transfers that can lag.

## Why the USDT price on P2P differs from the spot rate

Every ad carries the merchant's own price, so the ARS rate you see is a quote from a person, not a rate set by OKX. On Argentine peso pairs that quote usually sits above the converted spot price, and the gap reflects the friction of moving pesos in and out of crypto locally, including demand and banking constraints. Spreads tend to tighten during peak local trading hours when more ads compete.

That's a mechanism, not a forecast. Compare two or three ads for the rail you actually use before you commit, and remember that the number on the order screen at the moment you place it is the only price that counts under the 2026 rules.

## FAQ

**Does OKX P2P charge a fee in Argentina?**
No. OKX states that posting ads and buying or selling crypto on P2P are free, so the platform takes 0. Your bank or wallet may charge its own transfer costs, and a seller adding an undisclosed fee on top of the displayed price is violating OKX's rules.

**What's the minimum I can buy?**
The minimum ad size is 10 USD, so small test orders are possible. Larger orders above 15,000 USD require KYC level 3 with video verification.

**Can I pay with Mercado Pago?**
Mercado Pago appears on OKX's official list of Argentine payment methods, alongside Brubank, Uala, Lemon Cash, Paysur, Prex, bank transfer and cash in person. That list dates from December 2022, so confirm the method is available on the live order page before you commit.

**A seller asked me to add 2% after I placed the order. What do I do?**
Don't pay it. That request is one of the four prohibited behaviours under OKX's September 2026 rules, and it carries penalties from a first warning up to a permanent freeze. Decline, keep the conversation in OKX chat as evidence, and open a dispute if the seller refuses to release.

**What happens if I miss the payment window?**
The order cancels automatically and your completion rate drops. Three consecutive misses and you lose the option to cancel orders manually in future.

**Why is the P2P rate higher than the spot price?**
Merchants set their own prices, and peso pairs typically carry a premium that reflects local demand and the cost of moving fiat in and out of crypto. The premium moves through the day.
